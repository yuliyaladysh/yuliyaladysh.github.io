Chapter 1 · 2006–2014

One scholarship, four universities

A scholarship to study in Switzerland is the honest reason there are four universities on my path: not indecision, just one door opening onto the next.

  • Looking back, a very accurate spoiler for everything that followed.

  • Translating between English, Spanish and Russian in Minsk, then clinical trials in Warsaw. Useful training for explaining things to people who don’t share a vocabulary.

  • Which led to a joint MBA programme with INCAE, and a ticket to Costa Rica.

  • Plus a market-entry project for MASISA in Mexico City: research, go-to-market plan and ROI models.

  • The Swiss chapter, and the door into sport. Next stop: stadiums.

Chapter 2 · 9 events · 2010–2019

Stadiums, torches & running orders

For a few years my office moved with the calendar: wherever the big event was, that’s where the job was. The plan is lovely, and then you meet reality.

  • Organised corporate events and conferences, and represented the company at trade fairs. The warm-up act.

  • Co-organised the festival with the Secretary General, and coordinated Olympic Solidarity projects with the Olympic Committee President.

  • Researched global talent identification models and helped build criteria for spotting sports potential in school-aged children.

  • VIP guest services, daily agendas for Official Sponsors and Suppliers, and very organised guest lists.

  • Developed the Global Event Management System, digitising championship processes, and designed accreditation, credentials and event signage.

  • Volunteer training programmes and schedules for event services, plus problem-solving on the ground.

  • Same city, second Games: event services operations and reporting for senior management.

  • In between the big ones: market research, client relationships and a 30% increase in client acquisition in the first year in Lugano.

  • The McDonald’s Player Escort Programme, from planning and supplier tenders to live match days.

  • UNICEF was the Games’ only non-commercial social legacy partner. I led the partnership and grew the volunteer programme from 30 to 135.

  • Part of the same partnership, alongside marketing and communication campaigns around the Games.

Chapter 3 · 2020 onwards

Plot twist: from events to tech

Then the world closed its stadiums. During COVID I had to reinvent myself, so I learnt two programming languages, and that’s how my tech journey began.

  • if (world.isClosed) { learn("programming", 2); } Tests: passing ✓

  • System, regression and functional testing, with Jira, Test Rail and friends.

  • Designed and tested top-rated apps, including Filmic and Firstlight. From testing software to designing it.

  • Research, personas, wireframes, Figma prototypes and usability testing. Made it official.

  • Manual and automated test cases, regression testing and release validation.

Chapter 4 · 2026 – now

Making small businesses run themselves (a bit)

Since January 2026 I’ve helped small businesses in tourism, travel and import/export replace manual work with AI-powered operations, then train the team so the new tools actually stick.

  • Enquiries, quotes, bookings, suppliers, invoicing and follow-ups, automated end to end. One travel agency went from almost no systems to automated core processes.
